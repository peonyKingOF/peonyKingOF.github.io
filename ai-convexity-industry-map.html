The AI Convexity Map

Every industry scored on the three axes — so the shape of the prize is visible before any ticker is.

This is the layer above the stock screen. Each industry is scored on T, E, and I, and sorted into convexity bands. The organizing idea: E and I set how large the prize can get (uncapped output × value that scales with intelligence); T is orthogonal — it decides whether that prize actually reaches the institution, and in what shape. A high-T industry with low E/I is not convex; it's a one-time margin event. The hunting grounds are the cells that are high on E and I but not yet crowded.

Reading the map

Four things fall out of the scoring. (1) The richest cells are bio-adjacent — drug discovery, materials/chemistry, and precision diagnostics all pin both upside axes, which is why the framework predicts a sector-wide mania there. (2) The best risk-adjusted edge is in the under-crowded uncapped cells — materials & chemistry discovery, mineral exploration, cybersecurity insurgents, and agtech score like biotech on E/I but carry a fraction of the attention. (3) The corpus tier is a bet on a legal question — its I is genuine, but whether owners can charge AI tolls (vs being disintermediated) is unresolved copyright, so it's an event-driven convexity, not a compounding one. (4) The clearest shorts aren't the capped tier — they're the inverse tier: capped industries still get a real one-time margin lift, but IT-services/BPO literally sell the labor AI replaces, so the gains accrue to their customers.

The T trap

The seductive error is to reward high T. Lead-gen, horizontal SaaS, and IT services all restructure fast — but fast restructuring of a capped or inverse business just pulls forward a bounded (or negative) outcome. T only becomes an upside multiplier when it sits on top of high E and I. Read T last, not first.

Where this goes next

The natural drill-downs are the three high-E/I, low-crowding cells: materials & specialty chemistry (thinnest public pure-play coverage — worth a proper hunt), cybersecurity (incumbent-vs-AI-native capture question), and mineral exploration (best T of any uncapped cell). The corpus tier is worth a screen framed entirely around toll-ability rather than growth.

Method. Industries are scored 1–10 on each axis as qualitative applications of the framework in the attached note; there is no vendor data in this table by design (financials belong in the name-level screen). Bands are derived primarily from E and I, with T read as a separate realization/shape modifier. "Public exposure" names and the ETF column are illustrative anchors, not recommendations; most listed ETFs are impure proxies for the row, and where the cell says "none," no clean vehicle exists — an absence that is itself crowding information.
