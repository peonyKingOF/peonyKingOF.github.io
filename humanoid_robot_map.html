Who leads, and why — by axis, not by company

  • Mobility — belongs to the acrobats and the veteran: Boston Dynamics (self-righting, battery self-swap, two decades of dynamic locomotion), Unitree (3.3 m/s record, flips at consumer prices), EngineAI (world-first front-flip, most humanlike gait), Robot Era (4 m/s claimed). Note how little this axis predicts commercial rank — the mobility leaders and the deployment leaders barely overlap.
  • Manipulation — where the West still leads: Figure's 16-DoF tactile hands doing four-minute autonomous dishwasher cycles, 1X's 25-DoF tendon hands, Tesla's 22-DoF hand hardware, and Sanctuary's stranded 20-DoF hydraulic hands, now for sale without a robot attached. China's story is thinner: strong grippers and arm dynamics (Astribot's 10 m/s end-effector), weaker fine-motor autonomy.
  • Autonomy — the most punitively scored axis, because teleoperation dressed as autonomy is the sector's endemic disease. Credible highs: Figure (Helix — strongest, though largely company-published, evidence of long unattended runs), Galbot (independently visited unmanned stores), Toyota's TRI (frontier LBM work, verified on Atlas), AgiBot's GO series. Explicit penalties: 1X (teleoperators are the business model), Sanctuary (teleop-first methodology), Tesla (repeated post-hoc teleop reveals), and much of the Chinese demo circuit (CNN: rentals ship with a human operator at ¥3,500/day).
  • Maturity — Chinese: Unitree's 5,215 audited units and AgiBot's ~5,100 are together over half of 2025 global volume (IDC ~18,000) — though both fleets skew research and education, not labor. The most auditable Western maturity is Agility's: 65,000+ operating hours across nine facilities, stated in SEC filings.
  • Cost — a rout: Unitree and Noetix set a price floor ($1,400–$16k) no Western OEM approaches; 1X's $20k NEO is the only Western entry within an order of magnitude.
  • Traction — splits by kind: UBTech has the deepest verified factory roster (BYD, Foxconn, Zeekr, FAW-VW), Agility the most verified paid hours, Figure the marquee BMW deployment (40 units, per BMW itself) — while Tesla, XPeng, and the entire R&D tier have effectively none.
Section · form factor → use case → prize

Design Approaches

What the architecture choice commits you to — and which use cases pay.

  • Architecture is destiny on timing. Gripper-and-wheels designs monetize now but cap their ceiling; dexterity-first bipeds carry the largest theoretical prize and the longest, most capital-hungry road; dynamic bipeds win attention and research budgets while they search for a job that pays.
  • The safety constraint sorts the market. A 70–100 kg rigid industrial biped cannot safely share space with untrained people — physics confines it to factories, where the near-term money is. 1X's compliant tendon design is the only architecture that can live in homes, and its teleop reliance is the price of that softness.
  • The end-effector split is the sharpest divide. Hands are a bet on the full manual-labor TAM at brutal cost (the most failure-prone, hardest-to-control component, multiplying the AI burden); grippers are a bet on billable hours now. The only Western player with SEC-auditable operating hours (Agility) refused to build hands, and China's most-verified autonomous deployment (Galbot's stores) runs on grippers.
  • Wheels are winning the present. Verified commercial deployments skew heavily wheeled: half the bill of materials, none of the balance risk, 24/7 uptime. Legs are a bet that brownfield spaces — stairs, ladders, vehicle interiors — matter enough to pay their premium.
  • Watch the hedgers. AgiBot and Apptronik ship both wheeled and biped variants; Leju's chassis converts between the two. If one form factor wins decisively, the hedgers re-platform fastest.

Unit shares are analytical judgments against a 2025 global base of ~13,300–18,000 units (Omdia vs IDC). TAM figures are order-of-magnitude analytical estimates of the annual end-market each approach's use cases could support around 2035, anchored to published forecasts — Goldman $38B sector by 2035 (conservative), Morgan Stanley $4.7T by 2050 and Citi $7T by 2050 (bull paths that imply $150–300B+ by 2035 if home robotics works) — and to bottom-up ceilings (global factory labor cost ~$4T, 2B+ households). They are not precise and disagree with each other by design; the point is the rank order and the shape. Read the table right-to-left: today's unit-share leader (dynamic biped) has the smallest terminal market, while the two approaches with today's smallest volumes (compliant/home, dexterity-first) hold the only paths to the $100B+ pools — that inversion is the sector's core tension.

The Production Ledger

Maturity, year by year — actuals, targets, and whether to believe them.

  • Only three companies have audit-grade numbers. Unitree (5 → 410 → 5,500+ — the industry's only audited multi-year ramp), UBTech (1,079 — the only four-digit audited full-size figure), and Agility (~$37M revenue, 65,000 hours, in an SEC S-4). Everything else at volume is claimed or analyst-counted.
  • Nameplate capacity runs 30–100x ahead of output almost everywhere. Figure ~3% of its 12,000/yr line; Agility ~1% of RoboFab; Leju's brand-new 10,000/yr line has published nothing since opening. Winners will be whoever closes that gap — not whoever announces the biggest number.
  • The 2026 math doesn't add up. Global shipments ran ~2,300 (2024) → 13,000–18,000 (2025, Omdia vs IDC); the 2026 analyst cluster sits at ~50,000 (Morgan Stanley, Deutsche Bank, TrendForce). Summed company targets exceed that by a wide margin — someone's guidance is wrong, and the track record (Tesla −90% vs its 2025 target, Fourier −90% vs 2024) says whose.
  • How to read the grades: audited (prospectus / annual report / SEC) · 3rd-party verified (Omdia counts, press-toured lines, customer press releases) · company-claimed · speculative (targets with no track record, or contradicted by it).

Mispriced cells

Sort by CAP, then by MKT, and the divergences fall out. High CAP, weak MKT — the M&A shelf: XPeng's IRON (CAP 6.3 against MKT 2.7) is the sector's biggest gap, though it is captive inside a listed parent; the actionable versions are Robot Era (fastest legs, real VLA, thin order book), Astribot (world-class arm dynamics, no blue-chip customer), LimX (credible locomotion, modest traction), and Sanctuary (the best hands in the industry inside a distressed cap table — a components acquisition waiting to happen). The precedents are set: Mobileye paid $900M — 5.5x the last private mark — for pre-revenue Mentee, and Kelin's Kepler takeover shows what the distress exit looks like. High MKT, weak CAP — the story-risk shelf: Chery's Mornine (a 2-hour battery selling on consumer e-commerce) and Noetix (toy-price volume) monetize novelty, not labor. Hidden robot content in public tickers: Hyundai 005380.KS is priced as an automaker while owning the sector's best locomotion; Samsung 005930.KS holds Rainbow, a humanoid-specific solid-state battery program (SDI, H2 2027), and the foundry contract for Tesla's Optimus chips — three separate humanoid exposures inside one chaebol discount.

Method & disclaimer

Formulas. CAP = (MOB + MAN + AI) / 3 · MKT = (MAT + COST + TRAC) / 3 · Overall = CAP×0.40 + MKT×0.60 — equal factor weights within each axis, maturity-tilted overall. Scores 0–10 are analytical judgments applied to the raw metrics shown under each chip, not measurements. Teleop-dependent demos are penalized heavily on the AI factor. "(claim)" marks company-sourced numbers; "Not disclosed" marks unpublished data — the two are deliberately distinguishable.

Flagged uncertainties. (1) The 2025 volume crown is contested: Unitree's prospectus claims 32.4% share vs Omdia/Xinhua crediting AgiBot ~39% — methodology differs; both shipped ~5,100–5,500. (2) Nearly every speed, payload, and runtime figure in this table is company-claimed; no independent benchmark standard exists. (3) Nameplate capacity ≠ output: UBTech (6,000/yr), Leju (10,000/yr), Tesla (1M/yr) are all claims against far smaller audited deliveries. (4) Figure's 2024 BMW scope was disputed by Fortune (one robot, non-production hours) before BMW's own June 2026 confirmation of 40 units — treat all founder-sourced fleet numbers cautiously. (5) Apptronik's DoF specs conflict across sources (28–71); Dobot's likewise (28 vs 41). (6) Unitree's ticker does not exist yet; Agility's AGLT has not begun trading; AgiBot's HK IPO is unfiled-to-pending. (7) Hand-metric anchors held consistent with the companion hands table: Tesla 22-DoF, Figure 03 16-DoF/3g tactile, 1X 22+3-DoF, Unitree Dex5-1 16-active. (8) Sector context: ~330 Chinese humanoid models were unveiled in 2025; K-Scale, Aldebaran, and Embodied already shut down — consolidation is underway.
