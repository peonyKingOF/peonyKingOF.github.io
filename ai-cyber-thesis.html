Equity Thesis Map · Self-directed research

When the models get smarter,
the attack surface explodes.

Mapping public cyber names against a single dynamic: every capability jump in frontier LLMs simultaneously (a) arms attackers — automated exploit discovery, vuln research at scale, AI-written malware, volume social-engineering — and (b) creates brand-new surfaces to defend — prompt injection, model exfiltration, agentic misuse, data poisoning, an explosion of non-human identities. The same force is a tailwind for spend and a solvent for moats. This note scores both sides for each name and flags where the market has already agreed versus where it hasn't.

The backdrop you can't ignore: 2026's "AI eats software" re-rating

You're not looking at this group in a vacuum. Across Feb–Apr 2026 the market started pricing the threat half of this exact thesis — hard. The trigger was AI labs shipping the offensive capability directly: autonomous code-scanning-and-patching tools and frontier vuln-discovery models that found hundreds of unknown high-severity bugs in widely-used software. Investors read it as "if a general model can find and fix the holes, what am I paying the security vendor for?"

FEB 2026
Anthropic ships Claude Code Security (autonomous scan + patch). Cyber names fall ~10% over two sessions.
FEB 11, 2026
Palo Alto closes CyberArk (~$25B) — "secure human, machine & agentic identity."
MAR 11, 2026
Google closes Wiz ($32B, ~32× ARR) — cloud security pulled inside a hyperscaler.
APR 9, 2026
Frontier vuln-discovery model news triggers a ~$1T software/services drawdown.

The result: multiples compressed violently and unevenly. Zscaler trades ~63% below its 52-wk high while ARR still grows 25%; Rapid7 sits near a $0.45B market cap on ~2% growth. The bull rebuttal (Dan Ives' "AI ghost trade"; Arora calling the selloff "paranoid"; Kurtz: "an AI that scans code does not replace a battle-tested platform") is that more AI-written code = more vulnerabilities = more to secure and monitor — but only for vendors that pivot from watching humans to governing agents. That tension is the whole game, and it makes the priced-in vs. under-recognized distinction matter far more than the raw benefit score.

The distinction that matters to you

Where the thesis is already in the price

Priced-in — the market already agrees

  • Cloudflare (35.3×) — the cleanest AI-traffic beneficiary; held up best in the selloff. Benefit fully recognized.
  • CrowdStrike (33.5×) — agentic-SOC leadership is consensus; you're paying for perfect execution.
  • Datadog (21.9×) — "more to monitor" is priced, and it carries the most "AI-eats-software" risk of the three.
  • Palo Alto (18.5×) — the platform-winner narrative is well-known, but it's the most reasonably-priced mega-platform vs. how completely it owns the AI-security categories.

Under-recognized — benefit real, multiple compressed

  • Zscaler (6.6×) — 25% ARR growth + enforcement moat at a multiple the selloff cut indiscriminately.
  • SailPoint (6.4×) — agentic-identity leader growing ARR 28% / SaaS 38%, valued like a slow IAM vendor.
  • Rubrik (10.6×) — 48% growth for a DSPM/resilience play that's directly in-thesis.
  • Okta (6.4×) — real agent-identity optionality, discounted for ~12% current growth (the catch).

Don't confuse under-recognized with merely cheap: RPD (1.25×), TENB (3.2×), CHKP (5.0×), QLYS (5.4×) and S (4.7×) are low-multiple because the market has correctly identified their core jobs as the ones AI and the hyperscalers are compressing.

How I scored it & what I left out

Methodology, judgment calls & caveats

AI-benefit rank (1 = most strategic)

A qualitative composite — not a formula — weighing five factors:

  1. Structural TAM exposure — does AI escalation directly grow what they sell, vs. a passing tailwind?
  2. Shipped, monetizable AI-security product — design wins / ARR / bookings, not slideware.
  3. Category ownership — do they own a new AI-security category (agentic identity, AI runtime, AI traffic)?
  4. Consolidation position — winner-take-more platform, or share-donor?
  5. Moat durability vs. commoditization — enforcement/governance/recovery layers score higher than detection logic a model can absorb.

Threat band (Low / Med / High) is the mirror image: commoditization of their core job, hyperscaler consolidation, becoming an AI attack surface themselves, or attackers simply outpacing their model.
