Bio Data Acquisition Targets for Anthropic

Public companies with rich proprietary peptide and RNA datasets, ranked by attractiveness as acquisition targets · Valuations live as of July 7, 2026
Why this matters. Anthropic has moved decisively into biology: it launched Claude for Life Sciences (Oct 2025) and the Claude Science research workbench (July 2026, ~60 functions spanning genomics, proteomics, structural biology and cheminformatics), is running an internal drug-discovery program for neglected diseases, and has already acquired Coefficient Bio to bring wet-lab data generation in-house. The strategic gap is proprietary, ML-ready sequence→function data — exactly the kind of asset a foundation-model company cannot scrape from the public web. This screen ranks public peptide/RNA players on how valuable and acquirable their data would be for that purpose.

Scoring methodology

Each company scored 1–5 on four dimensions, combined into a weighted composite:

Data Value & relevance (35%) — depth, scale and peptide/RNA specificity of proprietary sequence/structure–activity data.

AI-readiness (25%) — how structured, labeled and model-training-ready the data already is (platform-native vs. clinical byproduct).

Acquisition feasibility (25%) — inverse of size/complexity; a sub-$3B data platform is buyable, a $40B commercial pharma is not.

Strategic fit (15%) — cleanliness of the data-first thesis vs. entanglement with clinical/commercial baggage or licensing encumbrances.

The central tension

The deepest peptide/RNA datasets sit inside companies that are effectively unacquirable at Anthropic's scale — Alnylam ($43B RNAi), Moderna ($32B mRNA), Ionis ($14B antisense). Buying them would mean buying a commercial pharma, not a dataset.

The most attractive targets are therefore the AI-native discovery platforms — Absci, Recursion, PeptiDream, Schrödinger, AbCellera — whose entire reason for existing is generating structured, model-ready biological data, and whose valuations sit in a buyable $0.3–2.4B range.

Note: PeptiDream and Zealand are non-US listings (Tokyo / Copenhagen); cross-border acquisition adds regulatory and political friction.
