What the grounded numbers say

"Strategic data" and "cheap stock" are mostly different lists.

Anchored on EV/Sales, the scarcest-data names — Reddit, Tempus, Guardant, Natera, Planet — trade at 12–30x sales. They're growth/optionality bets, not discrepancies. The real gaps sit in slow-growth information owners the market treats as AI victims: Gartner (~2x), IQVIA (~2.7x), plus distressed/levered Clarivate and Getty.

Live data killed one thesis and confirmed another.

Wiley was the cleanest "deep value" call — but IBKR shows it +44% YTD at its 52-week high, so the market already paid for the AI-licensing story; now Neutral. Gartner went the other way: live −37.6% YTD near its low, on a 77%-gross-margin franchise — discrepancy real and current.

Cheap-but-falling is not the same as cheap.

ZoomInfo has the lowest multiple (~1.7x) yet sits at all-time lows with negative growth — a falling knife, held at Neutral. Same caution on Definitive Healthcare. Low EV/Sales only helps when revenue isn't shrinking.

Convexity reshuffles the list one more time.

Discrepancy asks "is it cheap?"; convexity asks "is the payoff asymmetric?" — and the two only partly overlap. Gartner scores high on both (cheap quality + reversal optionality). But the steepest convexity sits in the distressed, option-like equities — Clarivate, Getty, ZoomInfo — where a small stub over a big asset (or a beaten-down base) means bounded loss against a multi-bagger if a catalyst hits. That's high convexity, not low risk: the left tail is fat. At the other end, the premium compounders (Moody's, S&P, MSCI, RELX, TRI) are low-convexity by construction — quality caps the downside but the price caps the upside. The genuinely interesting quadrant is high-data + high-convexity where the downside is a real business, not just a cheap option: Gartner, IQVIA, Planet, Tempus.
