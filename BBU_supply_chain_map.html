1. What a BBU is and why demand is exploding

A BBU is a lithium-ion battery module that sits inside the server rack (on the power shelf, typically 48–50V DC, moving to 800V DC architectures), providing seconds-to-minutes of ride-through power directly to IT load — replacing or supplementing room-level UPS. Nvidia's GB300 generation effectively standardized BBUs in AI racks to buffer the massive, spiky power draw of GPU clusters. The AI-server BBU market is estimated at ~$2.8B in 2026, growing ~28% CAGR, and hyperscalers (Amazon, Meta, Google, Microsoft) are procuring on 2-year contracts with volume escalators.

3. Where the bottlenecks lie

Bottleneck #2 — high-rate cylindrical cells (binding constraint)

BBUs need power cells (ultra-low impedance, 100A+ discharge, 200W+ per 21700 cell, stable at 35–45°C), not commodity energy cells. Only a handful of qualified suppliers exist: Molicel (E-One Moli, subsidiary of TCC 1101.TW) is the incumbent shipping its S Series to major CSPs; Samsung SDI only started mass production of its 40V3 BBU cell in Malaysia in July 2026; LG Energy Solution launched a 2170 BBU line; EVE Energy serves Chinese hyperscalers; CBAK is previewing high-rate LFP 26650s. EV/ESS cell capacity cannot simply be repurposed — qualification, chemistry, and line configuration differ. This is why pack makers like Dynapack and AES cite cell allocation as the gating factor while tripling pack capacity.

Bottleneck #3 — qualified pack/module makers

Hyperscaler and Nvidia-reference qualification takes 12–18 months, giving incumbents (AES-KY, Delta, LiteOn, Dynapack, Simplo, Celxpert, AcBel) a 1–2 generation moat and pricing power during the GB300+ ramp.

Bottleneck #1 — upstream refining

Lithium hydroxide and high-purity graphite are China-concentrated; a tail risk (export controls) rather than a current constraint. Least constrained: power semis, enclosures, and system integration — broad supplier bases.

5. How to think about convexity

The highest convexity sits where the bottleneck meets small revenue bases: AES-KY (purest at scale), Dynapack (capacity tripling on a $2.2B cap), and the two lottery-ticket micro-caps (Celxpert, CBAK). Simplo stands out on valuation (0.8x EV/S) with real Amazon/Meta BBU shipments. TCC is the contrarian way to own the actual bottleneck (Molicel cells) at a cement-conglomerate multiple — sum-of-parts story. The cell makers (SDI, LGES) get a narrative boost but BBU is small vs their EV business; the big integrators (Delta, Vertiv, Eaton) are AI-power plays where BBU per se moves the needle little — and Delta Thailand at 18x EV/S already prices in a lot.

Key risks: BBU content per rack could fall if 800VDC + facility-level ESS (grid-scale batteries) absorb the buffering role; supercap/Ni-Zn substitution; hyperscaler in-sourcing; Taiwan concentration risk (most of the pack ecosystem); FX; and rough exposure estimates — companies don't disclose BBU revenue cleanly.
