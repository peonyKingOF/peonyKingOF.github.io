The dominant lens on AI and equities is binary — winner or obsolete. This board replaces that with the three axes from the framework and scores every name on each, so a company reads as a shape, not a verdict. Rows are grouped the way the industry map is — by tier → industry → individual names — so the shape of the prize is visible before any ticker. The point the framework keeps making: a capped "winner" (Slack, a CRM) and an uncapped one can carry the same multiple while having wildly different convexity. Those are the rows to look at.

EEnd-market convexity weight 0.50

Is demand saturated (team chat, CRM seats, ad inventory) or theoretically unbounded (novel molecules, chip designs, undiscovered deposits)? High = uncapped output. Upside axis #1.

IIntelligence-level convexity weight 0.30

Does the asset's value rise with how smart the AI reading it is? The Reddit-vs-Nature axis. High = value compounds toward superintelligence; low = flat or decays. Upside axis #2.

TTranslatability weight 0.20

How short is the bridge from individual productivity to institutional productivity? Simple models, thin coordination layers, few products score high — the $ANGI profile. Modifier, not upside.

How to read the board

The framework's sharpest trade isn't a single name — it's a pair at the same multiple with different shapes. The canonical one from the note: $CRM and $SDGR both near ~3.9–4.3× EV/Sales, one capped on E, one uncapped. Scan the Cross-industry comp column for those pairings. A row with high T but low E/I (most of the short side) is a one-time margin story — real cash, bounded terminal value. A row with high E/I but a rich EV/Sales is paying up front for the convexity; the interesting ones are high E/I that still screen cheap.

Two honest pushbacks (carried from the note)

1) The translation bottleneck exists inside biotech too — an in-silico pipeline of 30 candidates still hits wet-lab and clinical-trial throughput, so bio's convexity has a longer duration than the market's patience (2024–25's post-hype cooling is the evidence). That's why the tools/CRO block scores well on E without the single-platform science risk. 2) Capped incumbents aren't dead money — margin-expansion-via-layoffs is front-loaded cash flow, so the △ short-side can outperform for uncomfortably long even with bounded terminal upside.
